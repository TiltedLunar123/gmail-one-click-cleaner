Gmail storage full? Here is what actually frees space

When the 15 GB runs out, Gmail stops accepting new mail and senders start getting bounces. The fix is not complicated, but the order matters, and one step gets left out of almost every guide, which is why people delete thousands of emails and watch the number refuse to move.

Written for the current Gmail and Google One, August 2026.

First, the thing that trips everyone up

Deleting mail does not free space. Emptying Trash does.

Deleted mail goes to Trash and sits there for 30 days, still counting against your quota the whole time. If you have just deleted ten thousand emails and the storage bar has not moved, this is why.

Open Trash, click Empty Trash now, then do the same in Spam. Then wait: the storage figure updates on Google's schedule, not yours, and it can take a few hours to catch up. Nothing is wrong when it lags.

Why it filled up

Your 15 GB is not a Gmail allowance. It is shared across Gmail, Google Drive and Google Photos, so a phone quietly backing up photos can fill the account of somebody who has never sent a large attachment in their life. Before touching your mail at all, check Google's own storage manager, which sorts your largest items across all three services and will tell you in about five seconds whether this is actually a Gmail problem.

If it is Gmail, it is almost never the number of emails. It is a small number of messages with big attachments. Twenty years of newsletters might be 200 MB. Forty video attachments from a family group thread can be more than that on their own.

The searches that find the space

Paste these into the Gmail search box. They are ordered by how much room they usually free per minute spent.

  • larger:25M The genuinely huge ones. Often twenty messages holding several GB.
  • larger:10M older_than:1y Big and old enough that nobody will miss it.
  • has:attachment larger:5M older_than:2y The long tail. This is where most accounts are actually hiding it.
  • in:anywhere larger:10M Same again, but including Trash, Spam and archived mail.
  • category:promotions older_than:1y Rarely much space, but it makes the mailbox usable again.

Once a search is on screen, tick the box at the top left to select the page, then click Select all conversations that match this search in the strip that appears. Without that second click you have only selected the fifty on screen, which is the other reason people think they deleted everything and nothing happened.

Sort by size instead

Gmail has no sort-by-size, which is the single most requested thing it does not do. The storage manager linked above is the closest official substitute: it lists your largest Gmail messages directly, and you can delete from there.

What to do, in order

  1. Check the storage manager to find out whether this is Gmail, Drive or Photos.
  2. If it is Photos, that is a separate job and usually the bigger win.
  3. In Gmail, work the larger: searches above, biggest first.
  4. Empty Trash. Then empty Spam.
  5. Wait a few hours before deciding it did not work.
  6. Unsubscribe from what refilled it, or you will be back here in a year.

Things that do not work

  • Archiving. Archived mail moves out of the Inbox and stays in All Mail, taking up exactly as much room as before. Archiving is for tidiness, not for storage.
  • Deleting without emptying Trash. Covered above, and worth repeating because it is the number one reason people give up.
  • Buying 100 GB and forgetting about it. That is $1.99 a month, about $20 a year, forever, to avoid an afternoon of cleanup. Sometimes that is the right trade. It is worth knowing you are making it.

Common questions

Why is my Gmail storage full when I have deleted everything?

Almost always because Trash has not been emptied. Deleted mail keeps its space for 30 days. Empty Trash and Spam, then allow a few hours for the number to update.

How do I find large emails in Gmail?

Search larger:10M, or larger:25M for the worst offenders. Add older_than:1y to limit it to mail old enough to be safe. Gmail cannot sort by size, so the size operators are the way.

What happens when Gmail storage is completely full?

You stop receiving mail, and people writing to you get a bounce message. You can usually still read and delete what is already there, which is what lets you dig yourself out.

Does archiving free up space?

No. Archived mail still lives in All Mail and still counts. Only deleting and then emptying Trash frees anything.

Is 15 GB just for Gmail?

No. It is shared with Google Drive and Google Photos, which is why the culprit is often not your mail at all.
